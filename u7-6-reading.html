Jamie 26

I always wanted to be a chef, but the teachers at my school thought it was a bad idea. My parents expected me to go to university – it didn't matter which subject I studied, so long as it was well respected. So I decided to do economics. I hated it and was getting bad grades. So, one of my lecturers suggested taking a year off. I decided to go to France and work in a restaurant. I learned so much in a two-year stay! Now I'm back home, I got a bank loan and I recently opened my own place. I work crazy hours, I'm not rich, but I don't care. I wish people wouldn't tell kids they can't do something. You can do anything if you really want to.

Anoushka 24

When I was growing up, my parents didn't have a lot of money, and life was sometimes difficult. They encouraged me to study hard at school so that one day I would be able to get a well-paid job. I had friends, but I didn't go out much. I was ambitious and self-confident, and I spent most of my time studying. At eighteen, I won a place at university to study law and moved away from my home town. Now I have a great job, but I work fifteen hours a day. I haven't tried to keep in touch with my old classmates, but I follow them on social media. Sometimes when I see them having fun, I wish I was more easy-going. I have a nice lifestyle, but I often feel lonely. Sometimes I wonder what I worked so hard for.

Leon 25

At school, I wasn't interested – and didn't sit any exams. After I left, I took any work I was offered – it was often boring and badly paid! But one job was gardening for an old guy, who was really well educated. Sometimes he'd chat to me and we talked about life and the world. Soon he began lending me books – and I remember thinking, there are all these ideas I didn't know about! It made me want to go back to school, then higher education. I gained a qualification as a social worker. Now I work with kids like me – it's a great job. I tell them how important education is – because for me, it was life-changing.
