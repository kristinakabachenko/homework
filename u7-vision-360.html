Real schools · Madrid, Spain

The Submarine School

In September 2022, a very unusual school opened on the edge of Madrid. Its students quickly gave it a nickname: ‘the submarine’, because of its round windows and its strange shape.

The Reggio School was designed by the architect Andrés Jaque. It follows the Reggio Emilia approach, a way of teaching that started in Italy in 1945. In this approach, children learn by exploring and asking questions, and the building itself is seen as a kind of ‘third teacher’.

That’s why nothing in the building is hidden. Pipes and cables are left in the open, so students can see how a building really works. In the middle of the school, there is a small indoor forest under a glass roof, and the classrooms for older students are higher up, around it.

The school also tries to be kind to nature. Its walls are covered with a thick layer of cork, which keeps the building warm, and tiny gardens on the outside walls attract birds, butterflies and even bats.

About 750 students, aged from two to eighteen, study here. And when the school’s owners first met the architect, they told him exactly what they wanted: magic.

Based on: Monocle, Architectural Record, Baunetz Wissen, ArchDaily
